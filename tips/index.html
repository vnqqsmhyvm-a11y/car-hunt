CarsBuying tipsModel guide

Before you buy any car

  1. Check the VIN for recalls. Ask the seller for the VIN (the 17-character vehicle ID). Type it into nhtsa.gov/recalls. It’s free. Any open recall should be fixed by a dealer, at no cost, before you buy.
  2. Get an independent inspection. Have a mechanic you choose (not the seller’s) look the car over before any money changes hands. A seller who won’t allow this is a red flag.
  3. Pull a Carfax or AutoCheck report. It shows the title status, reported accidents, the number of owners and the odometer history. A “Verify title” badge on this site means we couldn’t confirm the title yet.
  4. Get an insurance quote on that exact VIN. The insurance numbers on this site are estimates. Tell the insurer the car will be kept mostly in Fayetteville, because that can change the price.

Insurance figures are ESTIMATES built from published averages, NOT QUOTES. Real premiums depend on the insurer, her driving record, credit-based insurance score (allowed in OK), deductibles, the parents’ existing policy and discounts. Get actual quotes for the specific VIN before buying.

Hyundai and Kia: what to know

Checklist for any Hyundai or Kia

  1. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and the Hyundai/Kia owner site: all recalls closed (especially 25V099000/21V259000 engine, 22V304000 steering, 23V526000/23V531000 oil pump).
  2. Confirm push-button start or immobilizer, or anti-theft software done (sticker plus dealer record).
  3. Engine: no knocking, no oil-pressure light, and no oil top-ups needed between changes. Ask whether PNSS software is installed.
  4. Transmission: smooth IVT, no slip; avoid DCT trims.
  5. Service records with on-time oil changes, since a second owner needs them for warranty claims.
  6. Get an independent pre-purchase inspection and a Carfax/AutoCheck.
  7. Get an insurance quote on the VIN.

The verdict, in plain English

Bottom line

Some 2022+ Hyundai/Kia cars hold up on reliability, but none matches a Corolla/Civic on every count. Kia Forte 2022-24 (2.0 automatic, not the GT) matches them on reliability: J.D. Power's 2026 study ranks it #3 compact car behind Corolla and Civic, and Consumer Reports rates 2022-23 'more reliable' and 2024 'much more reliable'. But its crash ratings are clearly weaker: NHTSA 4 stars, and IIHS rates the newer front and side tests and the headlights Poor. Elantra 2023-24 (SE/SEL 2.0) is closest overall: NHTSA 5 stars, IIHS Top Safety Pick, standard immobilizer, no engine recall. But it is the priciest of the group to insure (~$3,240/yr est.).

Conditional OK

Kia Soul 2023 and Hyundai Venue 2022-23/2025: good reliability ratings (CR; Venue tied #3 small SUV in J.D. Power 2026), but NHTSA 4 stars and thinner IIHS results. Soul 2023 must have engine recall 25V099000 done. Elantra 2021-22: reliable per CR, but key-start SE cars may lack an immobilizer - only buy with the anti-theft software confirmed, or a push-button-start SEL.

Avoid

Kia Seltos 2021-2023 (engine piston-ring recall 25V099000, plus 21V259000 on 2021; CR rates 2021 'less reliable'; 2021-22 theft exposure; NHTSA 4 stars). Hyundai Kona 2024 (first year of redesign, CR 'less reliable'). Any 1.6-turbo dual-clutch (DCT) trim: Elantra/Kona N Line, Forte GT, Seltos 1.6T. Any 2011-2022 key-start Hyundai/Kia without the immobilizer software done.

Theft (the “Kia Boys” problem)

What happened

Key-start (non-push-button) Hyundais/Kias built without an engine immobilizer could be started with a USB cable. About 8.3 million cars, model years 2011-2022 (AP). Hyundai: immobilizers standard on all cars built after Nov 1, 2021. Kia: all 2022 models have one, from the start of the year or as a running change (Kia statement).

Affects these years?

Hyundai software campaign 993 covers certain 2021-2022 Elantra, 2018-2022 Kona, 2020-2021 Venue (key-start cars only). Kia: 2021-22 Seltos built 11/21/2019-2/28/2022 (event 676), 2020-22 Soul built 11/24/2018-12/11/2021 (event 679), Forte through 2021. So: Elantra 2023+, Kona 2023+, Venue 2022+, Forte 2022+, Soul 2023, Seltos 2023 should have immobilizers. 2021-22 Elantra/Kona and 2021-22 Seltos need a VIN check.

Fix

Free dealer software update (alarm-triggered ignition kill), window sticker, and later an ignition-cylinder protector. Check eligibility/completion at hyundaiantitheft.com or Kia's VIN tool (customercare.kiausa.com). Push-button-start cars are not affected.

Insurers

In early 2023 State Farm paused NEW policies on certain non-immobilizer Hyundai/Kia models/trims in some states (reported: GA, LA, OR, WA, PA) and Progressive limited sales in some markets (reported: Denver, St. Louis). Existing customers were not dropped, and State Farm allowed an exception if an agent verified an immobilizer. I found no report naming Oklahoma. A car with an immobilizer (or the software done) should insure normally. Get a quote on the exact VIN before buying.

Engines

Theta II / Nu failures

The big Theta II engine-failure recalls (2.0T/2.4 GDI) were Sonata/Optima/Santa Fe/Sorento/Sportage-class, not these models. The relevant one is the 2.0L Nu MPI piston-oil-ring defect: Hyundai 21V301000 (2019-20 Elantra, 2019-21 Kona/Veloster); Kia 21V259000 (2020-21 Soul, 2021 Seltos) and 25V099000 (2021-23 Seltos built to 7/1/2022, 2021-23 Soul built to 4/19/2022). Remedy: dealer inspects, replaces engine if needed, and installs PNSS knock-sensing software that triggers replacement if it detects damage.

2021+ Elantra / 2022+ Forte

These use the Smartstream 2.0 MPI. I found no engine recall or knock-sensor campaign on 2021-24 Elantra or 2022-24 Forte in NHTSA data (Elantra 2023 has recall 23V526000 for the Idle Stop&Go oil-pump controller; 2024 has 24V308000 EGR valve).

Transmissions

IVT (CVT-type)

Used on all 2.0/1.6 MPI versions (Elantra, Kona 2.0, Venue, Forte, Seltos 2.0, Soul). Kia campaign SC199 (TCM software and IVT replacement if needed, for slipping/delayed acceleration) covered 2019-20 Forte, 2020-21 Soul, 2021 Seltos built to April 2020. Nothing similar found for 2022+ IVTs. Check: smooth take-off, no flare/slip or shudder, no check-engine light; ask for SC199 completion on early cars.

7-speed DCT (1.6T)

Hyundai campaign T7E (input-shaft snap ring, 1-2 shift shock) on 2021-22 Kona 1.6T and 2021 Elantra 1.6T. Kia SA454 DCT logic update (limp mode) on 2021 Seltos 1.6T. DCTs are the most complaint-prone box here. Skip turbo/DCT trims for a teen.

Conventional automatic

The 2024 Kona 1.6T uses an 8-speed automatic, and Mazda uses a 6-speed automatic everywhere (no CVT), which is a plus.

Compared

Corolla and Civic use CVTs with good records in these years. Subaru: Impreza/Crosstrek use the TR580 CVT. Subaru extended the CVT warranty to 10yr/100k for 2019-20 Impreza and 2019 Crosstrek (bulletin 16-155-25R, 2025), and this transfers to later owners. 2021 Crosstrek has CVT inhibitor-switch recall 23V755000, and 2021 Impreza has CVT select-cable recall 21V024000.

Timing belt vs. chain

Hyundai/Kia

The 2.0 Smartstream/Nu MPI, 1.6 MPI (Venue) and 1.6T Gamma engines all use timing chains with no scheduled replacement interval. I found no chain or tensioner recall for these model-years. Concern about Hyundai/Kia timing belts applies to older engines, not these.

Others

Corolla (1.8/2.0), Civic (2.0/1.5T), Mazda Skyactiv-G, and Subaru FB20/FB25 also use chains with no interval. Subaru has a chain/sprocket service procedure bulletin for 2011-22 FB engines but no campaign. On every one of these, the real timing-chain risk is skipped oil changes, so get service records.

Warranty on a used car

Rule

Hyundai/Kia 10yr/100k powertrain is original-owner only. Later owners get 5yr/60k powertrain from the ORIGINAL in-service date (Hyundai/Kia warranty manuals). The 5yr/60k basic warranty also runs from that date.

What it means now

2021 cars (in service 2020-21): expired or about to (time). 2022: ends roughly late 2026-2027 or 60k miles. 2023: roughly 2027-28 or 60k. Most listings here are over 60k miles, so powertrain coverage is already gone. Examples with some left: 2023 Soul LX 32,760 mi, 2022 Venue SEL 30,898 mi, 2024 Forte/Elantra/Soul under 60k, and 2025 Venue (full basic warranty). Confirm the in-service date with a Hyundai/Kia dealer by VIN.

Reliability

Covers 2023 MY at 3 years, problems per 100 vehicles (lower is better): Subaru 181, Toyota 185, Kia 193, Hyundai 198, industry 204, Mazda 210, Honda 211. Compact car award: Corolla, Civic, Kia Forte. Small SUV: Crosstrek first; Venue tied third.
